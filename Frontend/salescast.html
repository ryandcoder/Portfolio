Overview

Businesses plan stock, staffing, and budgets around what they expect to sell. SalesCast takes historical sales, explains what has been happening, forecasts the coming months, and says what the numbers mean for the business.

My priorities were correct analysis first, a clear interface second, and complicated machine learning last. I kept the model simple enough to explain and tested it honestly.

What it does

  • Sales KPIs

    Headline figures that summarize overall sales performance.

  • Category, product, and region analysis

    See which parts of the business drive sales.

  • Trend and seasonality

    Trend lines with a 3-month moving average, plus the seasonal pattern in the data.

  • Forecast with a range

    Forecast for 3, 6, or 12 months (6 by default) with prediction intervals that show the uncertainty.

  • Filters

    Filter by date, category, product, or region. The forecast is labeled as overall or filtered so it is never misread.

  • Insights and recommendations

    Written from the data each time. None of it is hardcoded. An inventory signal is included and marked as informational only.

How the forecast is checked

The main model is Holt-Winters (exponential smoothing with trend and seasonality), using statsmodels. A forecast is only useful if you know how wrong it can be, so I tested it honestly:

  • The data is split 80/20 into training and test sets, and the model is scored on data it has not seen.
  • Accuracy is reported with MAE, RMSE, and MAPE.
  • The model is compared with a naive baseline, so a fancy method has to beat a simple guess to be trusted.
  • Forecasts include prediction intervals instead of a single confident line.

The data

SalesCast uses a synthetic sales dataset that I generated for this project, and the app says so. It has multi-year sales records with an order date, product, category, region, quantity, unit price, discount, and sales amount. I did not invent profit figures. Nothing in it describes a real business.

What I learned

  • How to evaluate a forecast fairly: a held-out test set, error measures, and a baseline to beat.
  • Why showing uncertainty with prediction intervals is more honest than one line.
  • Working with seasonality and trend in time-series data.
  • Being open about synthetic data and about which signals are only informational.
